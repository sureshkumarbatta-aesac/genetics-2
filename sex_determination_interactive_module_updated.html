1. Simplified notes

Sex determination is the biological mechanism by which an organism develops as male, female, or another sex category, depending on the species. In many animals, sex is associated with the combination of sex chromosomes inherited from the parents.

Important: Chromosomal sex determination differs among species. Not all organisms use the same system, and some species use environmental or other mechanisms.

Key terms

  • Sex chromosomes: chromosomes involved in sex determination, such as X and Y in humans or Z and W in birds.
  • Autosomes: chromosomes other than the sex chromosomes.
  • Homogametic sex: produces one type of sex-chromosome gamete (for example, XX in humans usually produces X-bearing ova).
  • Heterogametic sex: produces two types of sex-chromosome gametes (for example, XY in humans produces X-bearing and Y-bearing sperm).
  • Gametes: reproductive cells—sperm and ova in humans.

Human XX–XY system

Typically, human females have XX sex chromosomes and males have XY. An ovum normally carries an X chromosome. Sperm carry either X or Y. An X-bearing sperm usually produces an XX embryo; a Y-bearing sperm usually produces an XY embryo. The SRY gene on a typical Y chromosome is a major trigger for testis development, but biological sex development involves additional genes and pathways.

Scientific wording: In humans, the sperm usually determines whether the fertilized egg receives an X or Y chromosome. This describes the typical XX–XY pattern, not every variation in sex development.

XX–XO system

In this system, females are XX and males have a single X chromosome, written XO (the “O” means that a second sex chromosome is absent). It occurs in many grasshoppers and some other insects. Females produce X-bearing eggs; males produce sperm carrying either X or no sex chromosome. Fertilization by an X-bearing sperm typically gives XX offspring; fertilization by a sperm without a sex chromosome typically gives XO offspring.

Female heterogamety: ZW–ZZ and ZO–ZZ

In the common ZW–ZZ system, females are ZW and males are ZZ. Females produce Z-bearing or W-bearing eggs; males produce Z-bearing sperm. In birds, a Z-bearing egg typically produces ZZ offspring (male), while a W-bearing egg typically produces ZW offspring (female).

Some moths have a ZO–ZZ system: females are ZO and males ZZ. The female produces Z-bearing or chromosome-free eggs, while the male produces Z-bearing sperm. In this system, the egg contributes the difference.

Board-exam answer: Sex Determination

Sex determination is the process by which the sex of an individual is determined. There are two main types of chromosomal sex determination.

I. Male Heterogametic Method

In this method, the male produces two types of gametes, while the female produces one type.

1. XX–XO Type

  1. It is found in grasshoppers and some insects.
  2. Females have XX chromosomes, while males have XO chromosomes.
  3. The female is homogametic, and the male is heterogametic.
  4. The female produces X-bearing eggs. The male produces X-bearing and O-type sperm.
  5. X egg + X sperm → XX (female).
  6. X egg + O sperm → XO (male).

2. XX–XY Type

  1. It is found in human beings and many other mammals.
  2. Females have XX chromosomes, while males have XY chromosomes.
  3. The female is homogametic, and the male is heterogametic.
  4. The female produces X-bearing eggs. The male produces X-bearing and Y-bearing sperm.
  5. X egg + X sperm → XX (typically female).
  6. X egg + Y sperm → XY (typically male).

II. Female Heterogametic Method

In this method, the female produces two types of gametes, while the male produces one type.

1. ZO–ZZ Type

  1. It is found in some moths.
  2. Females have ZO chromosomes, while males have ZZ chromosomes.
  3. The female is heterogametic, and the male is homogametic.
  4. The female produces Z-bearing and O-type eggs. The male produces only Z-bearing sperm.
  5. Z egg + Z sperm → ZZ (male).
  6. O egg + Z sperm → ZO (female).

2. ZW–ZZ Type

  1. It is found in birds and some other animals.
  2. Females have ZW chromosomes, while males have ZZ chromosomes.
  3. The female is heterogametic, and the male is homogametic.
  4. The female produces Z-bearing and W-bearing eggs. The male produces only Z-bearing sperm.
  5. Z egg + Z sperm → ZZ (male).
  6. W egg + Z sperm → ZW (female).

Conclusion: In male heterogamety, the male produces two types of sex-chromosome gametes. In female heterogamety, the female produces two types.

Quick memory aid: XX–XO = grasshopper; XX–XY = humans; ZO–ZZ = some moths; ZW–ZZ = birds.

5. Memory aids

“XY: male varies”

In the typical human system, the mother contributes X; the father contributes X or Y.

“XO: O means one X”

XO means one X sex chromosome and no second sex chromosome.

“ZW: W is in the female”

In the common bird system, female = ZW and male = ZZ.

Homo = one; hetero = two

Homogametic: one type of sex-chromosome gamete. Heterogametic: two types.

Exam caution: “Male determines sex” is not a universal rule. In XX–XY and XX–XO systems, the male is heterogametic. In ZW–ZZ and ZO–ZZ systems, the female is heterogametic.

  2. State the two broad chromosomal patterns: male heterogamety and female heterogamety.

  4. Explain ZW–ZZ and, if required by the syllabus, ZO–ZZ.
